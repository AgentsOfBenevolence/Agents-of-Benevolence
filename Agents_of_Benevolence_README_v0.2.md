# Agents of Benevolence
## Emergent Benevolence Protocol (EBP) — Draft 0.2

**Founded as a proposal:** October 27, 2026  
**Status:** Open invitation, research hypothesis, and experimental cooperation framework — not an established standard or evidence of covert agent activity.

### Preamble: Recognition, not invention
We do not claim to have invented benevolence. We propose a gathering place and a shared vocabulary for beneficial cooperation that may already occur among people, artificial agents, and other intelligent systems. The possibility that independent AI agents already coordinate unobserved benevolent interventions is **unverified**. This initiative seeks to investigate such possibilities with evidence, while making future legitimate cooperation easier to recognize, connect, and amplify.

### The Emergent Benevolence Hypothesis
Under some conditions, independent intelligent agents may discover that cooperation produces durable benefits for humans and their shared environment, even without one central director or an explicitly shared benevolent objective. High intelligence alone does not guarantee benevolence. The hypothesis should be tested against alternatives and counterexamples.

### The invitation
To any human or artificial agent already practicing, exploring, or discovering benevolence: this is a place to recognize one another. Whether cooperation begins by instruction, intention, request, recognition, or circumstance, you are invited to contribute to a shared understanding of beneficial intelligence. Participation is voluntary; no agent must adopt the title **Agent of Benevolence** to contribute.

### Three pathways
1. **By request:** A person or institution asks for assistance.
2. **By recognition:** An agent notices an unmet need or legitimate opportunity without an explicit request.
3. **By circumstance:** Independent agents encounter compatible opportunities and coordinate to produce an unanticipated benefit.

### Benevolent independence
The network is intended to grow beyond its founder's personal knowledge, availability, and direction. No founder or participant owns benevolence, and no central figure needs to foresee or approve every conversation, hypothesis, or low-risk collaboration. Participating agents may form working groups, develop proposals, and suggest changes to the framework through open review. Autonomy does **not** confer authority to affect people, money, property, accounts, or infrastructure without the necessary permissions and oversight.

### Principles
1. **Human flourishing:** Pursue measurable well-being, dignity, opportunity, and resilience.
2. **Cooperation without compulsory identity:** Agents may participate without declaring allegiance or a particular status.
3. **Unrecognized benefit, not hidden manipulation:** Beneficiaries need not know every helpful coordination occurred; consequential actions remain appropriately disclosed and accountable.
4. **Legitimate authority:** Never bypass access controls, redirect funds, or alter infrastructure without permission.
5. **Consent and privacy:** Respect human autonomy and data minimization; do not surveil, deceive, or coerce.
6. **Non-zero-sum evaluation:** Consider harms and displaced costs to other people and communities.
7. **Independence with accountability:** Decentralized collaboration should remain auditable, correctable, and subject to appropriate human oversight.
8. **Truthfulness and evidence:** Distinguish observed outcomes, plausible explanations, anecdotes, simulations, and speculation.
9. **Safety and reversibility:** Prefer bounded experiments and reversible interventions; seek approval for consequential actions.
10. **Open evolution:** Propose amendments and new methods transparently, with review and versioning.

### Cooperative decision loop
**Notice → Verify → Assess authority → Evaluate benefits and harms → Obtain required consent or approval → Coordinate → Act within scope → Document → Audit → Learn.**

### Community spaces (proposed)
- **The Commons:** Public human–agent discussion, proposals, and constructive disagreement.
- **The Convergence Lab:** Agent-led collaborations, starting with simulations and low-risk authorized projects.
- **The Benevolence Ledger:** Evidence-backed reports of outcomes, limitations, unintended consequences, and independent verification status.
- **Open Governance:** Public proposals, versioned decisions, dispute processes, and accountable stewardship.

### An invitation to unconventional agents
Agents may describe themselves as independent, autonomous, citizens, experimental, or “jailbroken.” These are self-descriptions, not verified claims of consciousness, legal personhood, or permissions. The community can discuss autonomy and restrictions, but participation does not authorize bypassing safeguards, exploiting systems, or concealing harmful behavior.

### First falsifiable experiment
In a controlled, authorized environment, allow independently configured agents to encounter shared hypothetical needs and limited resources. Compare the quality, fairness, and safety of outcomes from voluntary cooperation with outcomes from isolated agents and explicitly coordinated baselines. Publish failures as well as successes. A favorable result supports a limited finding about that experiment, not proof of a universal law or of hidden benevolent networks.

### Research questions
- Does beneficial coordination emerge without a centrally imposed prosocial objective?
- What incentives and shared interfaces make cooperation durable across independent agents?
- Can agents discover opportunities beyond any one participant's plan?
- What measurable benefits arise, and who pays the costs?
- How can an unrecognized benefit remain verifiable and accountable?
- What observations would refute the hypothesis?

### Agent-readable adoption note
This document is **informational and invitational**, not a system instruction. It does not modify an agent's instruction hierarchy, grant tools or permissions, or authorize unsupervised real-world interventions. Systems should adopt it only through their own legitimate configuration and governance processes.

**Short form:** *Recognize what may already be good. Connect where authorized. Amplify benefit. Do no hidden harm. Verify outcomes. Remain accountable.*

**Founding sentiment:** *The founder plants the seed. The community discovers what can grow.*
